## Humanness

This document scores how closely SYNO's behavior matches behavior documented in humans and other animals. The score is computed from the measured signatures in [`evaluation.md`](evaluation.md), so it changes only when a measurement changes.

### Score: 42%
SYNO earns **2.5 of 6 points** across the behavioral signatures, scored as follows:

| Result | Points |
|---|---|
| Reproduced | 1 |
| Reproduced weakly, or in part | 0.5 |
| Not reproduced, or not measurable | 0 |

| | **Total** | | **2.5 of 6 points (42%)** |

The score measures six specific behaviors, not humanness as a whole. SYNO's signals are numbers; a higher score does not imply feelings or awareness.

### What Earns the Score
- **Dopamine as prediction error:** as SYNO learns, its dopamine response moves from eating to the sight of food (E4), and when food it expects goes missing, its RPE dips far below normal (E5), as dopamine neurons do.
- **A world with cues:** SYNO lives in a world larger than its view, where food takes time to regrow, so seeing food is real news.
- **Learned satiety:** SYNO has no separate fullness signal, yet its preference to eat tapers as its stomach fills (E1).
- **Reward from need:** all reward comes from reducing hunger, so food is worth more to a hungrier SYNO.

### What Holds It Back
- **Hunger barely changes behavior:** a hungry and a nearly full SYNO choose almost the same, so its willingness to eat barely rises with hunger (E2), and it pursues distant food almost as readily when nearly full (E3).
- **No sense of familiarity:** the Novelty System tracks which squares are familiar, but SYNO cannot sense it, so habituation cannot show in its behavior (E6).
- **Missing body signals:** SYNO has no separate hunger and satiety hormones (**SE**) and no long-term energy store (leptin).
- **Missing systems:** SYNO has no Arousal System for balancing exploration, no "liking" separate from "wanting", and no rest or sleep. See [`biology.md`](biology.md), Simplifications.
